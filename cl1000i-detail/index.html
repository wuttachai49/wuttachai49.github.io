Chapter 2

Measurement principle

The analyzer uses two immunoassay formats. Sandwich is for large molecules such as HCG and anti-HBc. Competitive is for small molecules such as FT4 and E2. Both use antibody-coated magnetic beads, an enzyme label, washing by magnetic separation, then a luminescence substrate read by the photometer.

Graphic drawn from: CL-1000i Operation manual §4.1 (Fig 4.1, 4.2: bead, sample, label, 37 °C incubation, dispersion) · SD-APP-35 Product PPT slides 13–15 (separation, substrate, photometer)

Read the result the right way round

  • Sandwich: more analyte in the sample binds more labelled antibody on the bead, so more light. Calibrator signals must rise with concentration.
  • Competitive: sample analyte and labelled antigen compete for the same bead antibody, so more analyte gives less light. Calibrator signals must fall with concentration.
  • This is exactly what the MON flag checks: "ascending sequence of sandwich method and descending sequence of competitive method". Calibrators loaded in the wrong order trigger it.

CL-1000i Operation manual §4.1, §16.4 (MON)

What the signal is

The photometer counts photons (CPS). The reaction principle is listed as AMPPD-ALP; the substrate is the "AMPPD" bottle, which is colloidal and must reach room temperature for at least 6 hours before use. The Effect check reads three blank cuvettes and the result must fall between 2500 and 6500 RLU (see chapter 8).

From signal to concentration

Quantitative: 4PLC

CPS = (P1 − P4) / (1 + (C / P2)P3) + P4

  • CPS is photon count, C is concentration, P1–P4 are the four curve parameters.
  • A master curve is made for every reagent lot from several calibrator levels over several analyzers and packed on the reagent box as a 2D barcode.
  • At the lab you run 1 to 3 point calibration. The software adjusts the master curve parameters with the algorithm set for that assay.
  • Two stages: factory pre-calibration (master curve) and real-calibration at the end user.

Qualitative: cutoff and COI

  • 1-point: Cutoff = a × CalCPS + b
  • 2-point: Cutoff = a × Cal1CPS + b × Cal2CPS + c
  • a, b, c are fixed per assay. The sample RLU is compared with the cutoff to call positive or negative.
  • Infectious-disease results carry flags: REAC positive, NREA negative, INDE gray area.

Drainage and fluidics

  • Waste outlet no less than 50 mm wide and no higher than 100 mm; waste tubing no longer than 5 m; with a waste tank the guide tube is at least 2 m.
  • Waste outlets 1 and 2 are routed separately. Never join them or connect them to another instrument's waste, or back-flow can damage the analyzer.
  • Do not fold or press the tubes. The sewer opening must be lower than the system's waste outlet.
  • The analyzer drains 3.1 L of liquid waste per hour. Empty the waste tank before and after testing.
  • Four fluid connections: wash buffer inlet, waste outlet 1, waste outlet 2, waste sensor.
Biohazard Wear gloves and lab coat, goggles if needed. Dispose of waste under local regulation.

Chapter 5

IQOQ and performance verification

Form FR-APP-18 records either an installation or an annual calibration / performance verification. It has ten numbered items plus a header and sign-off. This chapter says what to write in each box, where the number comes from on the instrument, and the limit.

Header and sign-off

  • Tick Installation or Annual calibration / Performance verification.
  • Customer name, department, instrument model (CL-1000i), analyzer serial number, distributor, install or verification date.
  • Basic acceptance block: SW version (read it from the software; the earlier reports show 0.2.17.41770 and 1.2.21.44667 as examples).
  • Close the form with the overall conclusion Pass or Fail, the "Other requirement" line (correlate ___ samples; reference system for correlation), then the application signature and the laboratory signature, each dated.
Who may run it The report notes say the whole validation should be done by an engineer authorized by Mindray, following the CL1000i Series Performance Validation Guide. The report proves the machine's status only at the time of validation.

Doing the System Check

  1. Utility → Maintenance → Diagnostics → System Diagnosis → System Check.
  2. Set the lane number (default 1) and put a rack in that lane.
  3. In rack position 1 dispense at least 1 mL of system check solution 1 (probe precision test). In position 2 dispense 2 mL of solution 2 (cleaning test).
  4. Tick the tests. Wash buffer test must run with the background test. Wash test must run with background, probe and cleaning tests. Cleaning test must run with background, wash and probe tests.
  5. Start; Exit when done.

CL-1000i Operation manual §14.7

Choosing and recording item 9

  • Pick one of the items the hospital cares about most. Preferably use different detection methods across the five columns.
  • Run 15 replicates. If you use the ServiceUser tool's FQC report, the repeatability codes are JZJMD-1 Test 1 low, -2 Test 1 high, -3 Test 2 low, -4 Test 2 high, -5 Test 3 low, -6 Test 3 high.
  • Past reports in your files used 10 replicates of FT3, FT4, TSH at low and high level and, for infectious disease, 5 replicates of HIV and HBsAg RLU with a %CV limit of 5 %.
If item 5 fails The form's remark says: check whether the substrate and its tubes are contaminated, and whether the sampling verticality and horizontal position are appropriate. The manual adds: a failed background test points to contaminated substrate, substrate dispensing or the photometer; a failed probe test also points to the reagent probe sampling system; failed wash or cleaning tests point to dispersion aspirating or dispensing.

6.2 Samples: prepare, tube, volume

Pretreatment

  • Centrifuge at 2000 g (glass tube) or 2200 g (plastic tube) for at least 10 min; 15–20 min preferred.
  • BD red-cap tube recommended. Clot time: 15–30 min with clot activator, 60–120 min in a blank tube.
  • Remove bubbles. Look for fibrin, clots, hemolysis, lipemia, icterus and deal with them.
  • Serum level above 1 cm, otherwise transfer to a microtube.
  • Tubes fit: diameter 12–16 mm, height 65–102 mm. Push the tube bottom to the rack bottom or it can collide inside the machine.

6.4 Quality control

Setup

  • Two control levels, one normal and one abnormal. Run QC after every calibration, reagent lot change, maintenance and instrument problem check (CL1000i-TH manual).
  • Import target values by scanning the 2D barcode inside the control box: QC → QC Setup → Import Control. Values can only be edited when status is Stop or Standby.
  • Set positions in Control Position; set rules in QC Setup → Rules. 1-3s is normally used; choose more Westgard rules to suit the lab. Control X and Y are the first and second material.
  • Third-party controls: Mindray controls are recommended for basic system performance. For third party, evaluate the target values first because of immuno specificity, then add manually: name, lot, expiry, position, chemistry, target, SD.

Reading results

  • L-J chart shows up to 31 points per screen, up to 1 year, 3 control materials per chart. Mark bad points with a comment (up to 100 characters); deleting a point only removes it from MEAN and CV, not from the data.
  • Twin-plot shows the last 10 runs of control X against control Y: random error versus systematic error by quadrant.
  • Out-of-rule results raise a message and sound in real time and show the rule symbol (22S, 31S, R4S, 10X).
  • Never use the Archive menu to back up the hard disk.

6.5 Running samples

Three ways to identify samples

  • Sequential: by sample ID order. Rack ID: you define rack number and tube position. Barcode: tube can sit in any position of any routine rack.
  • Only one mode at a time. Routine and STAT share it. Change it only in Standby or Stopped: Utility → System Setup → Instrument → Analysis Mode.
  • STAT lane: default lane 6, any of lanes 1–6 can be set, several allowed. Request STAT from the STAT icon or from Program → Sample → STAT; load on the STAT lane; Start.
  • More than 5 samples for the same assay: use Batch.
  • A finished sample's position must be released before reuse (Program → Sample → Release). Auto release is at a set hour; the CL1000i-TH manual notes midnight. With LIS barcode mode no manual release is needed.

Rerun and dilution

  • Only finished results can be rerun: Results → Current → Rerun (F5), load the rack on the rerun lane.
  • Predilution uses the fixed factors below. Off-line dilution takes any factor from 2 to 9999 for a sample you diluted by hand. Replicate repeats the test.
  • Auto rerun (repeat, dilute or reflex) is set per assay in Utility → Chemistries → Ref Range with a priority order.
  • Recall all rerun values with Recall Rerun Results; choose which one is reported with Set Default.
  • Result missing at the LIS? Check it on the analyzer first. It may be above the linearity range and need dilution and a repeat before it goes to the physician.

7.2 Clean dispersion probes and tubes

The four dispersion aspirate probes (A1–A4) remove unbound material in the magnetic separation (chapter 3). If they are dirty or clogged, wash solution is left behind and the result is affected; contaminated aspirate probes cause carryover; crystals on the dispersion dispensing probes make washing insufficient and the substrate volume inaccurate. All three jobs start from the same command.

Weekly: clean the removed probe

  1. Wipe the outside with gauze or alcohol cotton wet with ethanol. Clean the inside with the unclogging device.
  2. Fit the syringe tool (accessory kit) to the probe quick joint. Put the other end in a beaker of CD80 and push and pull the plunger more than 10 times.
  3. Lift the tip above the liquid and push the liquid out. Repeat in a beaker of deionized or distilled water: aspirate, then discharge into an empty beaker, at least 10 times.
  4. Disconnect from the syringe, swing the probe and tube to shake out the liquid, dry the outside with lint-free gauze. Keep the tube connection tight throughout.
  5. Repeat for the other probes. Dry them 1 hour in a clean ventilated place, then keep each in a clean sealed bag.

Operation manual §15.5.1 steps 13–17

After fitting and safety

  • After a weekly swap run Maintenance Command → Fluidic Prime, tick Dispersion Cycle, set the cycle to 5, Execute. Watch the probes while it primes: they must be fitted correctly with no tube leakage.
  • Do it only when system status is Incubation or Standby.
  • The probe tip is sharp: bent or damaged probe = replace at once, or results become unreliable. Wear gloves, lab coat, goggles if needed.
  • A loose guide tube or a leak: call customer service, because the leak can cause system failure.
  • Related automatic checks: Daily clean tests the probes for clogs, then soaks them in CD80 (chapter 8). Clogged probes there mean unclog, check the connector for leakage and the pump tube, then repeat Daily clean.

Chapter 8

Troubleshooting

The manual's method has five steps: an error occurs and is indicated; check the error log and component status; identify the error; carry out the fix; check that the fix worked.

How the analyzer tells you

  • Alarm tone (volume adjustable under System Setup, can be silenced), alarm message in the prompt area, colour on the Reagent, Utility and Alarm buttons, an alarm message box, a result flag (data alarm) and the error log.
  • Yellow is a warning. The message box is yellow for a common error (a warning, or an invalid test, reagent or sample).
  • Red is a serious warning or error. The box is red and you can only reboot or exit. If the machine stops, click the analyzer icon and press Home to return to standby. If it stays red, read the error log and call service.
  • Every error log has a help icon with description, causes and solutions. Alarm → Error Log can be searched by date, event ID, component and type. The Edit Log records deletes and edits of results and calibration factors.

Decode an error code

Type a code such as A54001 or C06001.

Diagnostics you can run

  • Photometer: dark current, photon count, DCF. Run all three after photometer parts are replaced; run photon count and DCF when QC is out of range or precision is abnormal.
  • Level sense: for "probe contacts no liquid" or "aspirates nothing" alarms once you have ruled out a clog. Put 2/3 tube of water in position 1, 20 consecutive runs.
  • Dispersion, substrate dispensing, wash buffer dispensing, system diagnosis under Utility → Maintenance → Diagnostics.

Stop and call service

  • Dark current, photon count or DCF diagnosis shows Fail.
  • Red alarm persists after Home and a restart of software and analyzer.
  • Repeated wash buffer bubble alarms (the sensor needs calibration by an engineer).
  • Waste bin button pressed without changing the bin, then a gripper collision.
  • Sample transport error three times; DCF fluctuating during daily clean.
