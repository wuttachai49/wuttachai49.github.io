How it works
  1. Calculated factor = result ÷ (upper limit × target %). Example: 850 ÷ (500 × 0.60) = 2.83×.
  2. Suggested factor: the next standard factor at or above it (2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 40, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000).
  3. Volumes (simple pipette values): sample ≈ final volume ÷ factor; diluent = sample × (factor − 1). It picks round whole-µL values (multiples of 100, 50, 25, 10 or 5) so the factor stays exact. The final volume may differ by up to 6% from what you entered, e.g. 175 µL + 350 µL = 525 µL for 3×.
  4. Expected reading = result ÷ factor.
  5. Reported result = instrument reading × factor.
Warnings
  • Expected reading above the upper limit, or below 10% of it → reconsider the factor.
  • Sample volume below your pipette limit → a two-step dilution is suggested.
  • Result already within range → no dilution needed.
Limits
  • The factor is an estimate from the first, out-of-range reading, which may itself be inaccurate. The real result can differ.
  • Check the assay's maximum validated dilution and the correct diluent for that test; this tool doesn't know your lab's limits.

How it works
  1. Volume per level = replicates × volume per test + dead volume.
  2. Levels: equally spaced mixtures of the low and high pools. Level k of n has a high-pool fraction of k ÷ (n − 1) (0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100% for 5 levels).
  3. Volumes: high-pool µL = volume per level × fraction, rounded to a whole µL; low-pool µL = volume per level − high-pool µL.
  4. Expected concentration = (high µL × high conc + low µL × low conc) ÷ volume per level, using the actual rounded volumes.
  5. Pool needed = sum of each pool's µL across all levels, plus 10% extra for pipetting loss.
Warnings
  • Any pipetted volume below your pipette limit → increase the volume per level.
  • High pool not above low pool → levels would not span a range.
Limits
  • Mixing assumes the pools are well mixed and share the same matrix. Prepare the pools from patient or QC material per your validation protocol (e.g. CLSI EP06).
  • Measure the actual high and low pools; expected values are only as good as those assigned concentrations.
  • This tool prepares the dilutions only; it does not evaluate linearity statistics.
